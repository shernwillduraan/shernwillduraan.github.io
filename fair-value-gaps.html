Fair Value Gaps

Study imbalances created by displacement and learn how Fair Value Gaps can become areas of interest.

LESSON 1

Understanding Fair Value Gaps

A Fair Value Gap is commonly identified as an imbalance across a three-candle sequence where price moves aggressively and leaves an inefficient area between candles. The FVG can become an area of interest for a later retracement.

PRACTICAL EXERCISE

Find bullish and bearish FVGs. Mark the gap boundaries and the displacement that created each one.

LESSON 2

Bullish & Bearish FVGs

Bullish FVGs form during strong upward displacement and bearish FVGs during strong downward displacement. The gap alone is not enough; consider location, liquidity and structure.

PRACTICAL EXERCISE

Mark five FVGs and classify them. Record surrounding structure and nearby liquidity.

LESSON 3

FVG Confluence

An FVG becomes more useful when aligned with liquidity, order blocks, structure and premium or discount. The goal is to filter for quality rather than trade every gap.

PRACTICAL EXERCISE

Choose one FVG setup and build the complete narrative: higher-timeframe location, liquidity event, displacement, FVG, entry and invalidation.
